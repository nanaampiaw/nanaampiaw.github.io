Looking Ahead

My interests are becoming more connected as I gain experience. Electronics and embedded systems give me ways to measure and control physical processes; energy projects give me a reason to apply those skills. I’m especially interested in work where those areas meet, such as monitoring energy use, integrating sensors into equipment, and improving the operation of renewable-energy systems.

For my next internship, I want to work alongside engineers who can show me how a design moves from a useful idea to something that can be tested and relied on. I would like to contribute through hardware work, data analysis, or system integration while getting better at documenting decisions and explaining results. My research and field internship have given me a starting point, and I want to build on both.

The solar tracker is one place where I’m continuing that process. It brings together light sensing, mechanical movement, and embedded control. The purpose is to improve how a panel follows sunlight, but I also need to distinguish the intended benefit from what the prototype can actually demonstrate. Documenting the behavior of the system is part of the work, not just something to add at the end.

My experience in Ghana remains part of why I care about energy access and sustainable infrastructure. I want the skills I develop to be useful in settings where available resources and maintenance needs strongly influence whether a solution lasts. Studying in the United States and Scotland has broadened the approaches I can draw from, while keeping me aware that each setting has its own priorities.

I don’t have every step of my career decided. I know that I want to keep working close to both the technical details and the people affected by them. For now, that means building a stronger foundation, asking better questions, and taking on projects where I can be clear about what I contributed, what worked, and what still needs improvement.
