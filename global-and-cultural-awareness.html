Global And Cultural Awareness

Growing up in Ghana, studying in the United States, and spending time in Scotland have given me different reference points for the same questions. How do people use the resources available to them? What do they expect from infrastructure? Which problems receive attention first? I don’t assume that an approach that works well in one setting will work equally well in another.

Ghana is an important part of how I think about engineering. Seeing differences in infrastructure and access to resources has made me value resourcefulness and practical design. It also reminds me to ask whose experience is being represented when a problem is defined. The people who use a system may understand its limitations in ways that are not obvious from a drawing or a set of measurements.

In Scotland, my learning extended beyond the engineering visits. Edinburgh, Glencoe, the Isle of Lewis, Oban, Balmoral Castle, St Andrews Links, and Dundee exposed me to urban, rural, and coastal settings within the same country. Learning about traditional knitting and gin production helped me consider how local skills, industries, and identity connect with economic and environmental choices.

Visits to the Falkirk Wheel, Whitelee Wind Farm, and Cruachan Hydro Station added a technical dimension to that experience. Together with the Management and Sustainability course, they encouraged me to look at infrastructure in its surroundings: the resources it uses, the purpose it serves, and the decisions needed to support it over time.

I bring these comparisons back to my work at Bucknell. In a team, I want to understand why someone approaches a problem differently before deciding that one method is better. I’m still learning how to do that well, especially when explaining technical ideas to people whose experience is different from mine.

Activities

Outside engineering, football is a big part of my life. I enjoy playing, watching matches, and playing FIFA with friends from different backgrounds. It gives us something to share without needing to have the same academic interests or experiences.

I also enjoy photography. During travel and everyday life, I like looking for a view or a moment worth keeping. The photographs here include engineering visits, landscapes, time with friends, and other parts of my experience. Together, they show more of what I noticed and enjoyed than a list of places could.

Having interests outside the classroom matters to me. Sport, friends, and trying something unfamiliar give me room to step away from coursework and return to it with a fresh perspective.
