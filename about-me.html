Background

I’m Nana Kwame Owusu-Ampiaw, an Electrical Engineering student at Bucknell University. I’m interested in the part of engineering where a circuit, a program, and a physical device have to work together. That interest has taken me from building an Arduino-operated dustbin to integrating sensors on a remote-controlled car and exploring how a building could use energy more efficiently.

Growing up in Ghana shaped the questions I bring to this work. Differences in infrastructure and access to resources made me aware that a useful design has to fit the place where it will be used. As I study electronics and energy systems, I keep coming back to reliability, accessibility, and the practical work involved in keeping a system running.

At Bucknell, I’ve been able to approach those questions through both hardware and computation. My embedded-systems work has involved PCB assembly, wiring, sensor integration, and debugging. In research with the Civil Engineering Department, I used Python to simulate diffusion in porous media and compare numerical results with analytical solutions. Those are different kinds of work, but both require me to check what a result actually means before trusting it.

My internship at Thomas V. Giel Garage Doors added another perspective. I assisted technicians with automated systems, calibrated safety sensors, and programmed operator controls. It gave me a practical connection between the electronics I study and equipment that people depend on in everyday life.

I’m still developing my direction within electrical engineering, with a particular interest in renewable energy, power systems, and embedded hardware. I want to become someone who can contribute to the calculations and design while also understanding the testing, installation, and maintenance that follow.
